# Rust port of the power planet

One Rust codebase built two ways:

- **Web:** compiled to WebAssembly. It draws into the existing canvas on highskore.com, and the HTML HUD stays.
- **Widget:** compiled natively inside the Idle FighterZ widget (Tauri). It draws through wgpu (or softbuffer) instead of a web view's canvas.

**The Rust engine is the game.** The site page runs it by default: the top of the page starts fetching and compiling the wasm build while the page, its 3D title and intro start, and once it is in planet-core runs the game, drawn by planet-render, behind the HTML HUD ([HUD.md](HUD.md)); a card that is on screen (or pressed) before then shows a ki-charging dragon ball.

**game.js is frozen.** It (with `shenron.js`) is the legacy fallback: `dist/planet-game.js`, fetched only where WebAssembly can't run, if the engine fails to load, or with `?js` (for comparison), and still the widget's web-view build until the widget draws natively by default. Every new feature is built once, here in `rust/`; CI fails a pull request that touches game.js without the `legacy-js` label or a `Legacy-JS: <why>` commit trailer. page.html (the HUD) and world.js (the stage it hands the engine) stay shared. `npm run bench` compares the two engines in Chromium and WebKit ([test/bench/README.md](../test/bench/README.md)).

## Workspace

```
rust/
  Cargo.toml          workspace
  planet-core/        game state, simulation, economy, events, sprite/DSL data, deterministic fixed step
  planet-render/      software framebuffer raster shared by both targets (pixel-identical to world.js/game.js)
  planet-web/         wasm-bindgen entry: canvas putImageData, input, HUD bridge to page.html
  planet-native/      wgpu/softbuffer surface for the Tauri widget
  difftest/           headless JS harness + scenarios: the scripts golden/ replays, and the optional JS diff
  golden/             recorded Rust traces (per-frame hashes and state samples) the regression tests check
```

Dependencies only point downward: `web` and `native` depend on `render`, and `render` depends on `core`. `planet-core` has no dependencies and never touches pixels. It can run headless in tests, on a server, or in a worker.

### planet-core

- `Game` holds `Save` (game.js's `S`) plus the runtime closure state from `mount()`: `phi`, `laps`, `sprint`, `combo`, the timed buffs, `session` and so on.
- `Game::frame(t, dt)` mirrors `frame(t, dt)` statement for statement. Expressions keep JS operand order, so floats round the same way, and the diff test shows they come out bit-identical.
- **Fixed step.** game.js runs a variable step (`gdt <= 0.05`, at about 30 fps). The port runs a fixed `DT = 1/30`, with `Clock::advance` banking real time. That makes runs replayable and lets the web and native builds agree. In play the difference can't be seen, and the diff harness drives JS at the same fixed step.
- **RNG.** `Lcg` is the harness's `Math.random` stand-in. game.js spends random draws on cosmetic particles (a transform, a crit, kaioken, every `burst`, the space pod's trail). The core spawns those particles itself into `Game::fx`, at game.js's spots and in its order, so the gameplay stream stays aligned and planet-render draws them; a burst at a spot only the drawing places (a ghost, a movie henchman) burns its draws unplaced headless; the screen-shake jitter is drawn for real, since it moves the hero's screen spot. Once JS parity no longer matters (after cut-over), cosmetic draws can move to a separate render-side generator.
- `jsmath`: V8's `Math.sin`, `cos`, `atan2` (fdlibm) and `hypot` (Kahan), bit for bit. V8 for arm64 is built with clang's FP contraction, so the fdlibm port fuses multiply-adds in the same places; it matches node on arm64 over 400k random arguments. wasm has no fused multiply-add, so there `jsmath::fma` emulates it exactly (the unfused sum where that provably is the fused one, else Dekker and rounding to odd), tested against wasm's own software fma under wasm32-wasip1. On an x86-64 node the geometry may come out one ulp off (the samples log it as inexact).
- `geom`: the view matrix with its auto spin, the road and cap points, `layout()`, and where the hero lands on screen (`kidScreen`, `handPos`).
- `num`: JS `Math.round`, `toFixed`, `toExponential` and `toLocaleString`, plus `fmtShort` and page.html's `fmt` and `short`. They are exact, including round-half-up ties.
- `big`: `Big`, the number power and every economy quantity that can grow without bound are kept in (power, best, rates, multipliers, costs, ki seeds, villain hp, prizes). Below 1e300 it is a plain f64 and every operation is the f64 one, bit for bit; at or past 1e300 it is m × 10^e (1 ≤ m < 10, an integer exponent that saturates at 10^18), computed with IEEE basic operations, a table of correctly rounded powers of ten and `jsmath`'s log and pow, so it is the same on wasm, arm64 and x86-64. Nothing in the economy is capped for the double's sake any more (the old 1e300 power and 1e200 multiplier caps are gone); the ki ceiling and the other gameplay caps stay. Past 1e300 the formatters switch from their suffixes to e-notation, the exponent grouped and then short: `1.23e308`, `4.50e1,204`, `7.1e1.2M`.
- `data`: tables (shop, forms and hero ladders, the training tree, angels, goals) and tuning constants. The sprites are in `sprite` (stage 1), as plain data, so `planet-render` only rasterises.
- Outputs to the UI are data, not callbacks: `Game::events` (`ui.event` text) and the timeline's state in `Game::tl` (the dragon's offer in `tl.shen`, what's in the sky, the boss). Stage 4 adds a `HudState` snapshot (what `ui.update` builds today) and the shop rows (`syncShop`).

### planet-render

- **The perf crate is folded in.** px.wasm's kernels (`planet`, `outline`, `rim`) live in `planet-render/src/px_kernels.rs`; `wasm/src/lib.rs` keeps its exports, statics and `alloc` and `include!`s that file, and `px.wasm` rebuilds byte for byte. `planet-render/src/px.rs` includes the same file over a `Framebuffer` (the kernels keep px.wasm's statics, so calls take a lock). There is no `Raster` trait any more: one source, one owner.
- `Framebuffer`: `buf` (packed ABGR, ImageData's layout), `zb`, `ib`, `dm`, `st.px` blending, `PK`, `bayer`, `h2`.
- `scene::Scene::draw(fb, game, view, dt)` is game.js's `frame()` from `// ── draw` to `st.flush()`, in game.js's statement order, spending `Math.random` draws where game.js does (particles), and writing back the screen spots game.js writes while drawing (the hero's lift and facing, piccolo, bubbles, every visitor's `sx`/`sy`, the boss's and the ginyu force's `x`/`y`/`k`, henchmen, the capsule's fall, `ape.k`, the meteors' spin seed). `View` says site or widget (`BARE`), the widget's cloud shape and amount, and the pixel scale.
- Modules: `tones` (each world's palettes), `scenery` (`scenery()` for every world: spheres, the future's slabs, west city's streets, paozu's river, residents), `shade` (`shadePlanet`, `shadeKame`, `shadeW2`, `shadeTop`, domes, capsule corp, pools), `visitors` (everyone on and over the planet, the boss, the ginyu force, the movies), `effects` (spirit bomb, beam struggle, capsule), `beam` (the charge, its aim line and crackle, and the fired beam), `top` (the arena's void, stands and zeno's box), `snake` (snake way: the road, the cloud rows, orbs, spines and ghosts, king kai's planet and the arrival), `shenron` (shenron.js: the dragon's body and heads, porunga), `storm` (shenron's summon as a storm, new in Rust: planet-core's `dusk` is its timeline and the page's colours), `xform` (the transformation scene, below).
- planet-core gained what the drawing needs: `sprite` (`makeSprite`, the `shapeSprite` DSL, `makeHero`, and every other sprite baked by `difftest/sprites.js` into `data/sprites.bin`), `fx` (one particle list both sides add to), `flash_t`, `night_k`, `zap`, hit flashes, the dance's look, the movie wobble, `Game::preview(hash)` (the preview hooks) and `Game::set_view`. `jsmath` gained `pow` and `exp` as V8 computes them.

### planet-web

- `Planet` is planet-core's `Bridge` exported with wasm-bindgen: inputs, the shop, tree and angels in; the HUD's per-frame delta message and the menus out, as JSON in game.js's shapes. [HUD.md](HUD.md) has the API; the native widget uses the same one over Tauri IPC.
- page.html keeps its HUD, menus and shop. `rust/planet-web/shim.js` hands it a `game` object with game.js's API and maps the bridge's messages onto its `ui.*` callbacks. planet-render draws: the shim's frame is `frame_drawn(t, dt)` with page.html's own `t` and `dt`, then `putImageData` of the framebuffer, straight from the module's memory.
- Saves stay in `localStorage` under the same `hs-power` key and JSON shape, so a returning player keeps their daily-bonus date.

### planet-native

- `Widget<S: Surface>` runs the fixed-step clock and presents the framebuffer. The surface is wgpu, with softbuffer as a fallback; in either case it's a texture upload of the same RGBA buffer.
- HUD: Tauri overlays a small transparent web view carrying page.html's HUD markup and the same JS shim, talking to Rust over Tauri commands and events instead of wasm calls. Once the game runs natively, we can decide whether the HUD moves to Rust too (immediate-mode text with the 3×5 pixel font that's already in world.js) or stays HTML.

## Testing

**Golden traces are the regression test.** game.js is frozen and Rust will gain what it doesn't have, so the tests that must pass check Rust against itself: `planet-render/tests/golden.rs` replays each of the 18 scenarios' scripts (`golden/<name>.inputs.json`: the inputs game.js's drawn run applied, frame by frame, written by `golden/script.mjs`) with planet-render drawing every frame, and compares with `golden/<name>.trace`, a line per simulated second: the world, the form, the power, the random draws, a hash of the whole state sample (all the difftest samples below: the timeline, the economy, upgrades and tree, the HUD snapshot, the shop, the dragon's offer, that second's toasts and cues, and every ten seconds the save and the menus), and an FNV-1a hash of each of its 30 frames. 441,000 frames in all, 3.9 MB of text (2.3 MB gzipped); about 4 CPU minutes. When recorded, every frame hash was game.js's. A change that means to change what the game does or draws re-records them with `npm run golden:update` and commits the traces with it, so review sees which scenarios moved and from which second; a failure names the first second and frame that differ. The scripts don't change when Rust's behaviour does (a scripted input that no longer applies does nothing); a new scenario needs a script (from run.js, or written by hand).

**Differential testing against game.js** was the safety net of the port, and stays runnable while behaviour is game.js's: it's opt-in (`JS_PARITY=1`, `npm run difftest:js`), expected to pass only for frozen behaviour, and CI runs the headless one without failing the run. `rust/difftest/harness.js` mounts world.js, shenron.js and game.js headless in a `vm`, using the same LCG `Math.random`, and never draws. `run.js <scenario>` runs a scripted policy: training taps, a cheapest-first buy loop, kaioken, tree learning, ascending, and test-only state pokes for late-game setups. It prints:

- the inputs it actually applied, frame by frame
- a state sample every simulated second

`planet-core/tests/difftest.rs` replays the inputs in Rust and compares each sample. The sample covers power, best, `rate()`, `wrate()`, `mult()` with and without timed buffs, laps, phi, sprint, combo, form, mastery, seeds, the goal and its progress, every upgrade level and cost, every tree rank, and the RNG draw count. Counts must match exactly; floats are allowed a relative error of 1e-9. With `JS_PARITY=1` `cargo test` runs node; otherwise (or without node) those tests skip.

Stage 3 split the harness's timeline patch into a gate per system (31 spawn points), mirrored them in planet-core, and removed them one commit at a time, each diffing bit-identical. Now the whole of `frame()` is diffed with no patches. The sample also covers the timeline: the world, sagas, universes, wishes, bosses, trips, the boss's and the ginyu force's hp, the dragon's phase, how many orbs, meteors, saibamen and ki blasts are out, the space pod, the nimbus, the moon, the great ape, the tournament, movies, the storm, snake way, gregory, dr. gero, the dance, the struggle, the view matrix and the screen shake.

Scenarios: `idle`, `active`, `ascend` and `late` from stage 2, and three that drive the timeline. `events` is 45 minutes of active play that also taps whatever game.js can hit-test without drawing (the space pod, meteors, the moon, dr. gero) and trains its tail. `multiverse` opens the tournament of power through ultra instinct, clears universe 9, takes god ki to bring zeno, and erases. `wishes` summons the dragon every 50 seconds (porunga on namek), wishes krillin back, and picks every other offer by hand, leaving the rest to the 20-second auto-pick. Stage 4 added three: `input` drags the planet (and lets it coast), charges and fires beams (final flashes at beam lv 5) and throws big bangs at whatever fights, taps, cancels and trains with space; `snake` takes a pod to snake way and presses and keys through it; `daily` starts from yesterday's save; `calm` plays with reduced motion (no screen shake, no draw for it). `attacks` and `walls` fire the same pointer beams and big bangs at villains, the ginyu force and universe walls, with and without kaioken and the great ape. `itx` buys instant transmission at yardrat and tries its gestures (two-thumb taps, right-clicks, thumbs too far apart or sliding) and jumps from its picker every second, home or away, in a fight or not; it fails on its own if a jump doesn't land or moves the natural trips, if the cooldown from a jumped trip's end isn't exactly 300 s at lv 1 and the 90 s floor at lv 40, if a jump goes when it can't or is refused silently, if a natural trip comes within 84 s of a jumped one getting home, or if two thumbs in a beam struggle aren't taps. `toptaste` reaches the top form while the dragon offers a taste of the next one, then picks it by hand and leaves it to the auto-pick; it fails on its own if the form runs past the top or the game stops. `sides` plays the side upgrades past their old caps: crits past 100% chance, senzu past full uptime, high-level beams and big bangs, tail training past lv 10. Inputs: `train`, `buy`, `kaio`, `learn`, `set`, `click` (x, y), `summon`, `pick` (a wish key), `down`/`move`/`up` (CSS and buffer pixels, the pointer, a touch), `jump` (a world from the last transmission picker), `cancel`, `key`, `angel`, `auto`, `hero` (the angels' and characters' tabs, in `multiverse`).

Every scenario also checks what reaches the HUD: the `ui.update` snapshot (with `ui.night`, `ui.world` and the body classes) and the shop every second, the dragon's offer while it waits, the menu, angels, confirm boxes and share card every ten seconds, every toast (text, time and duration) and every cue (`ui.sfx`, `ui.fx`, `bump`, `achieved`, `zeno`, `treeGift`, `wish`, `wishDone`, `itxPick`, `notify`: call, time and arguments) in order, and the save (`JSON.stringify(S)`) every ten seconds. `save_round_trip` writes a save from planet-core, loads it in game.js and back.

The harness never draws, so the screen spots game.js writes while drawing stay unset: npcs' `sx`/`sy`, a boss's `x`/`y`/`k`, movie henchmen, the capsule's fall, `piccoloAt`, `monkeyAt`, `liftNow`/`flipNow`. planet-core keeps them as fields the renderer fills (`Seen`, `Boss::x`, `Game::lift_now` and so on), unset headless, so the two agree. game.js also changes the game in five places while drawing: the big bang lands, a great ape's stomp shakes the screen, a full beam charge makes it tremble while the hero is in view, and a boss's capsule falls and opens itself after 8s. The harness runs those headless too, and planet-core runs them in `Game::draw_step` after the frame, so they're diffed. The renderer should leave them there. The flip side: taps on things only the renderer places (king kai, gregory, yamcha, the car, the door, senzu beans, satan, roshi, pudding, gadgets, the birds, zeno, saibamen, bosses, ginyu members, movie henchmen) are ported but not diffed, and a boss's ki blasts fly at (0, 0) as they do in a headless game.js.

Framebuffer diffing (stage 1): `difftest/frame.js` mounts game.js with a preview hook at a canvas size (site or widget, with its cloud shape), runs it headless, clears the cosmetic state headless game.js never steps (particles, the flash), and draws the last frames (`__skip = false`). `planet-render/tests/framediff.rs` does the same in Rust and compares the RGBA bytes; `FRAMEDIFF=<name>` filters cases, `FRAMEDIFF_PNG=1` writes the JS, Rust and diff images of failing ones. `--px` loads px.wasm into the harness, to check its kernels against the JS loops. `planet-render/tests/drawn.rs` runs the state difftest's scenarios with drawing on, on both sides (`run.js <scenario>+draw`); drawn, the script also taps what only drawing places, so the renderer-placed taps are diffed there. The scenarios' replay is shared from `planet-core/tests/replay`.

## Rust-only features

- **The transformation scene** (`planet-core/src/xform.rs`, `planet-render/src/xform.rs`). A form-up is a scene, not one frame: for 7 s (3.5 s with reduced motion) he powers up on top of the planet, which turns to face us. The sky darkens behind it, one giant アア! shout sits behind the planet, cropped by the card and shaking harder as the power builds. Rocks tear loose and rise, lightning strikes, and his aura, which doubles as the tap meter, swells and kicks out with every tap. A column of light punches up out of him. Then the form lands: the flash, a sunburst in the form's colours for 3 s (1.5 s), the rocks flung away, and the form's name in the usual toast. While he powers up every tap goes into it (+2% each); 6 or more earn a power surge for 20 s, +1 in the timed buffs' sum (×2 on its own, as the boost's is), named in the same toast and in the buffs line. Idle, it lands on its own. On snake way, and for a form a preview hook or a test's poke of `S` sets up, it lands at once, as before. `#xform` opens on the scene.
